Abstract

Agriculture faces challenges such as crop diseases, inefficient irrigation, water scarcity and changing weather. This project builds a software-only AI assistant that lets farmers upload crop or leaf images and enter basic information such as crop type, growth stage, soil type and location. AI-based image analysis detects crop diseases, while a smart irrigation module uses crop information, soil characteristics, irrigation history and weather forecasts to recommend irrigation timing and approximate water requirements. The system also gives treatment and fertilizer guidance through an interactive web dashboard, without requiring physical sensors or IoT hardware.

Keywords: Smart Agriculture, Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Computer Vision, Crop Disease Detection, Smart Irrigation, Weather Forecasting, Water Management, Predictive Analytics.

Methodology

Disease detection

Images are preprocessed (orientation fix, resize to 256 px, centre-crop 224 × 224, normalisation) and classified by a MobileNetV2 network fine-tuned on PlantVillage (54,000+ images, 38 classes). The model is evaluated with accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and a confusion matrix. Predictions are combined with the farmer's selected crop; low-confidence results trigger a request for a clearer photo.

Recommendation engine

A curated knowledge base maps each class to symptoms, favourable weather, cultural, organic and chemical treatment, fertilizer and prevention. Weather profiles for each disease estimate infection risk from the forecast, and a spray advisory picks a window without rain or strong wind.

Smart irrigation (FAO-56)

Crop water use is ETc = Kc × ET₀, with ET₀ from the weather API and Kc from the crop and growth stage. The root-zone store is TAW = available water capacity × root depth, and stress begins after RAW = p × TAW is used. A daily water balance runs from the last irrigation date through the 7-day forecast, adding effective rainfall. Irrigation is due when depletion reaches RAW, and is held when useful rain is likely within 2 days.

Energy estimate (SDG 7)

Gross water depth = net depth ÷ method efficiency. Pump discharge is estimated from pump power and head, giving run time, kWh, cost and CO₂ (0.71 kg/kWh, Indian grid). Savings are measured against flood irrigation and against irrigating before rain.

Relevance to SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy

Irrigation pumping is energy-intensive. By recommending irrigation only when the soil needs it, holding irrigation before rain, quantifying the savings of drip and sprinkler systems, and rating solar pumping potential from forecast sunshine, the assistant helps farmers cut electricity and diesel use, lower costs and reduce emissions.
