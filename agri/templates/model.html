What happens to a photo
  1. Correct phone orientation (EXIF) and convert to RGB.
  2. Quality check: resolution, brightness, and sharpness (variance of the Laplacian).
  3. Resize the short side to 256 px, centre-crop 224 × 224, normalise.
  4. MobileNetV2 forward pass and softmax over 38 classes.
  5. Crop-context step: if the top class belongs to a different crop than selected, re-rank within the selected crop.
  6. Confidence below 50% is reported as uncertain and the farmer is asked for a clearer photo.

Pretrained weights: {{ info.source }} (Hugging Face), fine-tuned from Google's MobileNetV2 on the Kaggle "New Plant Diseases" version of PlantVillage. Retrain with ml/train.py.

Where it struggles

  • PlantVillage photos show single leaves on plain backgrounds. Accuracy is lower on field photos with cluttered backgrounds; Mohanty et al. (2016) reported a large drop on images from other sources.
  • Crops without a photo model receive irrigation, fertilizer and the common-problems guide only.
  • Results are decision support, not a laboratory diagnosis. Confirm serious cases with an agriculture expert.

How it works

  1. The PlantVillage MobileNetV2 turns each photo (and its mirror image) into a 1,280-number leaf description.
  2. A small classifier ({{ em.classifier }}) trained on the new datasets reads that description.
  3. The crop you select limits the answer to that crop's classes, the same way as the first model.

Reusing the leaf-trained network means the new classifier trains in minutes on a laptop and adds almost no time per photo. Retrain with ml/fetch_agml.py then ml/train_extra.py.

Limits to know

  • The rice dataset has no healthy leaves, so for rice the model always names the closest disease. Treat a rice result as "which disease is most likely" rather than "is it diseased".
  • Most photos were taken in fields in India and Bangladesh. Accuracy will drop on very different lighting or varieties.
  • Some source datasets include several photos of the same leaf, which can make test scores look better than real-world use.
